A Companion Study Guide

Start here

Three questions.

Philippians 4:13 is quoted more than almost any verse in the Bible. It gets printed on shirts, painted on walls, and said out loud before hard things. But the strength in it is not general. It is tied to a person, and that person came here on a mission.

Understand the mission and the verse stops being a slogan. So this guide asks three questions, and answers each one from the text before it explains anything.

The What

Bring God’s people back to Him.

His mission. What He came to do.

The How

Teach them to repent, obey God, love others, and follow His example.

His method. How He did it.

The Why

So they can become like their Father and inherit His Kingdom.

His purpose. Why it matters.

How to use this guide

Read the verse first. Sit with it before you read the explanation under it. That order is deliberate, and it is the whole method: authority comes from the text, never from opinion.

Every verse in this guide is quoted from the 1611 King James Bible. Words in [brackets] are the translators’ own supplied words, kept exactly as printed.

The Mission of ChristAnything Is Possible™

Part One

The What

His mission: to bring God’s people back to Him.

“For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:10

A shepherd with a hundred sheep does not shrug when one wanders off. He leaves the ninety and nine and goes after the one. That is the posture the whole mission is built on. Not waiting to be found. Going to find.

He came to seek the lost

Seeking is the first movement. Before any teaching, before any correction, there is a search.

He came to fulfil, not to destroy

“Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.”

Matthew 5:17

The mission is continuous with everything God had already said. He did not arrive to replace the standard. He arrived to complete it.

He came to give His life

“For God so loved the world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

John 3:16

“Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”

John 14:6

Seeking, fulfilling, and giving. In one sentence: to reconcile people to God by calling them to repentance, teaching them to obey God, and making a way for eternal life through His sacrifice.

Part Two

The How

His method: teach them to repent, obey God, love others, and follow His example.

“From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”

Matthew 4:17

The very first word of the public ministry is repent. Not a footnote. The opening instruction.

Five steps, in order

  1. Repent. Turn. Change direction. Everything downstream depends on this one.
  2. Believe God. Not believe about Him. Take Him at His word.
  3. Learn God’s word. You cannot obey what you have never read.
  4. Live it. Obedience is the evidence, and He said so plainly.
  5. Help others do the same. The method is designed to be handed on.

“If ye love me, keep my commandments.”

John 14:15

Love and obedience are not two separate things here. One proves the other.

“Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, [even] unto the end of the world. Amen.”

Matthew 28:19–20

Note what is commanded: not merely to inform people, but to teach them to observe. The goal was always a changed life, not a full head.

In one sentence: teach the truth, live the truth, invite people to repent, and make disciples who will do the same.

Part Three

The Why

His purpose: so they can become like their Father, glorify Him, and inherit His Kingdom.

A father does not correct a son to win an argument. He corrects him so the son grows into someone who carries the family name well. That is the shape of this last question.

To restore the relationship

“That they all may be one; as thou, Father, [art] in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me.”

John 17:21

To transform the character

“Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.”

Matthew 5:48

To prepare us for the Kingdom

“From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand.”

Matthew 4:17

The same verse that opened the method also names the destination. The turning and the Kingdom are one subject.

To glorify the Father

“For I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of him that sent me.”

John 6:38

Every part of the mission points back to the One who sent Him. Which is where Philippians 4:13 finally lands: the strength was never yours on its own, and it was never meant to be.

Work it through

Reflection questions

Answer honestly. Write in the margins. This page is meant to be used, not kept clean.

On The What

  1. Luke 19:10 says He came to seek. Where were you when you were sought?
  2. Matthew 5:17 says He came to fulfil, not destroy. What have you been taught about the law that this verse corrects?
  3. If the mission was to bring people back to God, who is currently far off that you have stopped going after?

On The How

  1. His first public word was repent. What is the specific thing you already know you need to turn from?
  2. Of the five steps — repent, believe, learn, live, help others — which one are you actually stuck on?
  3. John 14:15 ties love to keeping commandments. Where does your obedience contradict what you say you believe?
  4. Matthew 28:20 says teach them to observe, not just to know. Who are you teaching by how you live?

On The Why

  1. Matthew 5:48 sets the standard as the Father Himself. Does that read to you as pressure or as purpose? Why?
  2. John 6:38 — He came to do the will of the One who sent Him. Whose will are you actually doing this week?
  3. If the Kingdom is the destination, what are you carrying that will not make the trip?

A prayer

Father, I have read what your Son came to do. Thank you for sending Him to seek me before I ever looked for you.

Teach me to repent, and not just to feel sorry. Give me a hunger for your word, and the honesty to live what I find in it. Where I have said I love you and not kept your commandments, forgive me and change me.

Make me like my Father. Use my life to bring somebody else back to you. And when the work is harder than I am, remind me that the strength was never mine — that I can do all things through Christ, which strengtheneth me.

In Yeshua’s name. Amen.
